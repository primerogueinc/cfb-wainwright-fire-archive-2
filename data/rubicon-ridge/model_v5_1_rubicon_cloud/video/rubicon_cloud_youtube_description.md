## Description

On Oct. 17, 2026, a grass fire on the ranges at CFB Wainwright, Alberta, turned when a cold front arrived. Four soldiers died. DND said 39 were injured (as of its Oct. 22 briefing).

This video follows the air response using only public data. It starts just before the front reaches the fire, shows satellite fire power and Prime Rogue's working-model estimate of how the fire spread, then follows the civil air-ambulance flights that public receivers heard that evening and overnight.

What the public data shows:
- The cold front reached the fire at about 3:28 p.m. MDT (working model v5). GOES-18 fire power went from 76 MW at 3:10 p.m. to 1,215 MW at 3:30 p.m.
- Working model v5 puts the fire at Range 2 at about 3:39 p.m. and Range 15 at about 3:42 p.m. These are working-model estimates (10–90%: 3:34–3:58 p.m.). DND said the incident happened "just before 4:00".
- From 3:34 to 5:20 p.m., no aircraft at or below 12,000 ft was heard within 80 km of the fire (106 minutes).
- The first inbound air-ambulance launches were a King Air (C-FSUG, operator not identified) climbing out of the Lac La Biche area at about 4:55 p.m. (estimated) and a STARS helicopter (C-FLCI, STAR03) lifting off at Edmonton International at 4:58 p.m. Both reached the Wainwright area at about 5:35–5:37 p.m. (estimated).
- From about 7:08 p.m. to about 4 a.m., aircraft shuttled between Wainwright and Edmonton, Calgary and Saskatoon: 14 arrivals and 15 departures in the Wainwright area.

DND, Oct. 22 briefing: "They were cared for first in Wainwright, then flown to hospital across Alberta by STARS, Alberta Health Services paramedics, and Royal Canadian Air Force helicopters."

What this data can't show
- Receivers heard nothing below about 4,250 ft (about 2,000 ft above the ground), so landings and low hops are invisible.
- Military aircraft don't broadcast, so the RCAF helicopters DND mentioned are not visible.
- Landing and take-off times near Wainwright are estimates (±3–5 min).
- Operators are inferred from public call-sign prefixes (STARS; Can West Corporate Air Charters; Saskatchewan Government Air Ambulance Service). The operators of C-FSUG and C-GWRS are not identified. Nothing here says who was aboard any aircraft.
